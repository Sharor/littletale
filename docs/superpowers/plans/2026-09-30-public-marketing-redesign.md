# Public marketing redesign plan

Status: proposed scope and direction; implementation has not started.
Date: 2026-09-30
Audience: parents creating stories for their children, confirmed by the owner.
Language: English first, confirmed by the owner. Do not translate Danish or Greek until the owner has approved both the English copy and the redesign. Preserve existing locale behavior in the meantime.

## Intent and approach

Help parents understand the product, see the quality of its stories, understand the offer, and feel ready to create their first story. Rework the existing Rails public views and translations around a clear parent benefit and an actual story sample. Reuse the storybook design system. Measure landing visits through new-account creation and first completed book; use paid conversion as a downstream measure. Treat improvements as hypotheses to validate, not promised conversion gains.

## Scope boundaries

In scope:

- Landing-page structure, navigation, headings, benefits, process explanation, offer, FAQs, final action, and footer.
- Three curated, browsable English example books generated by the owner on localhost admin user 1, clearly labeled as examples and readable before sign-in. Feature one as the main landing-page sample.
- Public sign-in screens, authentication error/return messaging, and presentation of the logged-out gift invitation.
- Consistent public branding, page titles, descriptions, social sharing metadata, and English copy. Adapt Danish and Greek only after approval of the English copy and redesign.
- Public access to verified product/privacy information and approved legal documents through read-only presentation.
- Responsive styling, accessibility, asset performance, and a small conversion measurement plan using existing tracking where sufficient.

Out of scope:

- Changes to story generation, the signed-in library/editor, onboarding steps after authentication, or the sidebar design.
- New authentication providers, payment models, subscription entitlements, print fulfillment, PDF export, or a public user-book library.
- A CMS, blog/content program, advertising campaign, analytics platform replacement, or experimentation framework.
- Invented testimonials, customer counts, press coverage, development/learning benefits, privacy guarantees, or generation-time promises.
- Rewriting legal obligations or publishing/deploying this planning work.

## Proposed messaging and design

Lead with shared reading and personal relevance. Two alternatives are a photo-transformation-led page, which demonstrates personalization but needs more explanation of the finished book, and a gift-led page, which fits occasional purchases but is less aligned with the selected parent audience. Recommend shared reading as the main promise, with personalization as visible evidence and gifting as a secondary benefit.

Working English hero copy, subject to the claim review and owner approval:

> **Their next adventure starts with them.**
>
> Create an illustrated story starring your child. Add a photo or describe their character, choose what happens, and read the story together online.
>
> Primary action: **Create your child's story**
>
> Secondary action: **Read a sample story**

Use a short, accurate trial explanation beside the primary action after confirming the published offer. Explain AI assistance in the process and FAQs. Keep promises about likeness and illustration consistency proportionate to demonstrated output.

Proposed page sequence:

1. Compact navigation: sample story, how it works, offer/FAQ, and sign in.
2. Hero: parent benefit, clear digital format, primary action, trial summary, and an actual illustrated spread.
3. Samples: a featured story with readable text and consecutive illustrated pages, plus links to the other curated example books. Visitors can browse each complete short sample without signing in; label all three as examples.
4. Process: create a character from a photo or description, choose the story and style, then read together once generation finishes.
5. Personalization examples: familiar people, the child's interests, art styles, and supported languages. Curate a few relevant themes from `categories.md`.
6. Offer: trial limits, duration of reading access, and verified purchase/subscription options.
7. FAQs: format, photos, AI assistance, generation time, languages, trial expiry, and available support. Use verified answers.
8. Final creation action and footer with working contact and approved policy links. Add real social proof or the founder's story only where supported.

Design direction: a warm illustrated storybook with generous paper-colored space, readable story text, and an asymmetric desktop hero. Use EB Garamond for display text, Inter for body/actions, and `--fable-wine` for actions, focus, and accents. Reuse semantic public components and shared ink, muted, paper, gold, and line tokens. On phones, put the promise and main action before the sample. Keep motion optional and subtle; ensure the sample works without hover or animation.

Sign-in copy direction: “Create your first story, or return to your library,” followed by configured provider buttons and concise account-creation guidance. Preserve the return path for gift recipients and existing users.

## Example-book creation and owner handoff

Browsable examples are a planned deliverable. They let parents inspect the actual story and illustration quality before creating an account. Start with three short English books showing a bedtime adventure, a child-led exploration, and a story about friendship or family. Choose the final concepts after the audience/age brief, and keep their length representative of the advertised trial.

1. **Prepare the generation briefs.** The assistant supplies a title, named characters with ages, appearance and personality descriptions, and a paste-ready plot for each book. Include the intended reader age, page count, and an available illustration style so the owner can generate them through the existing app.
2. **Ask the owner to generate them.** Present the briefs and explicitly ask: “Please generate these example books using your current localhost admin account (user ID 1), then tell me when they are ready and provide their book IDs or local URLs.” The owner performs generation; the assistant must not silently generate substitute books or initiate paid generation calls.
3. **Wait for the completion handoff.** Once the owner confirms the books are ready, inspect only the identified books belonging to local user 1 and check that their text and illustrations are complete. Continue independent research and English copy work while waiting; leave extraction and sample-dependent design work pending. If a sample needs another generation, describe the issue and ask the owner to handle it.
4. **Extract the selected examples.** Use read-only access to the existing localhost development data and attachments to copy the selected titles, ordered page text, and illustrations into curated public sample assets/content. Preserve the source books and records. Do not recreate the database, expose the admin account/library, or include unrelated account data or original uploads. Record source book IDs for maintenance outside the public presentation.
5. **Integrate and review the samples.** Reuse the existing reader presentation where practical, with anonymous, read-only browsing and clear previous/next controls on phone, desktop, and keyboard. Serve curated copies so public visitors do not need access to the private source books or trigger generation. Include the real samples in the English redesign review; Danish and Greek versions remain on hold until the owner approves the English copy and redesign.

The owner has offered this generation/extraction workflow. The required future question is the concrete generation request in step 2, with complete character and plot briefs supplied first.

## Validation, risks, and open decisions

- **Comprehension:** a parent should be able to explain what they get, what makes it personal, how to start, what it costs, and how long trial access lasts. Use a small moderated review if participants are available; it is qualitative feedback, not proof of conversion lift.
- **Conversion:** define new-account conversion as new accounts attributable to eligible landing visitors divided by those visitors, with an agreed attribution window. Define activation as those new accounts completing a first book within seven days. Track paid conversion separately. Exclude returning-user sign-ins, bots, and test traffic; segment gift-entry visitors separately. Audit the existing Visitor/Event linkage before treating reports as reliable.
- **Tests:** run focused home, sample-reader, session, localization, navigation, visitor-tracking, and affected gift tests, followed by the full Rails suite. Prepare only the isolated test database if required with `bin/rails db:prepare RAILS_ENV=test`. No schema change is expected.
- **Rendering:** inspect 375px, 768px, and 1440px widths, long Greek/Danish text, 200% zoom, keyboard navigation, visible focus, contrast, reduced motion, and missing/slow-loading images. Confirm the sidebar and adjacent signed-in pages retain their established styling.
- **Performance and SEO:** compare landing-page asset weight and lab performance before/after under the same conditions; compress sample images, reserve their dimensions, and defer below-fold assets. Check public titles/descriptions, document language, sharing image, and indexing behavior for sign-in and tokenized gift pages.
- **Public information:** the existing terms controller participates in onboarding and updates tutorial state. Do not reuse that action as a passive public policy endpoint. Present approved documents through a read-only route if needed; do not imply that a referenced but unavailable privacy policy already exists.
- **Offer:** the actual paid amount/currency comes from Stripe configuration and was not verified in this audit. Agree the public price source before displaying numbers; do not hard-code an invented price or change billing to fit the copy.
- **Proof and assets:** confirm rights/consent for public photos and book samples, testimonial authenticity, and any founder claims. Never expose private customer books as demonstration content. For book examples, use the owner-generated selections from localhost admin user 1 via the handoff above. If social-proof evidence is unavailable, use these samples and factual product explanation.
- **Open owner decisions:** final public brand spelling, target market, priority child age range, confirmation of the trial/paid offer, support contact, approved policy text, and the final sample briefs/source book IDs. The owner will generate the examples on localhost admin user 1. English is the confirmed lead language; Danish and Greek translations must wait for approval of the English copy and redesign. Resolve the remaining decisions during discovery; they do not require changing application code now.

Stop condition: the approved public journey is implemented with verified claims, three complete browsable example books extracted through the owner handoff, complete translations, functional actions, passing focused/full tests, recorded phone/desktop checks, and a defined measurement/rollout plan. Further product features require separate scope.
